*** Diction
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: diction
:END:
Every word has a denotation - dictionary definition Only some words have
connotation - ideas, feelings, and impressions Diction synonyms:
lexicon, language

Gold: - Denotation: Yellow metal, chemical element of atomic number 79 -
Connotation: Wealth, luxury, prestige, perfection

Examples of diction: Animalistic, music, religious

Use an adjective before stating diction as diction itself is
meaningless. Also applies to tone, atmosphere, and mood.

“The town was an infested den of thieves and smugglers”

The author of the quote employs animalistic diction of “infested” and
“den” to imply that the “thieves and smugglers” are like animals,
dehumanising them in the process. The animalistic diction “infested” is
often associated with animals like rodents and pests, this evokes a
sense of disgust in the reader, and creates a feral atmosphere.
Therefore, the author has deployed animalistic diction to dehumanise and
evoke digust within the reader to

Model response: The author characterises “the town” to be “infested”
with criminals. Here, the deliberate use of animalistic diction in
“infested” serves to dehumanise the “thieves and smugglers” as creatures
comparable to cockroaches or rates, which evokes a sense of disgust in
readers. The animalistic diction thus captures the squalid, corrupted
state of this “town” and builds an unsettling atmosphere.

*** Modality
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: modality
:END:
*Modality* is measure of certainty Modality can characterise a character
High modality can create an authoritative and certain tone Low modality
creates an uncertain tone.

Shakespear utilises low modality in Macbeth's internal monologue to
convey an uncertain tone to capture his internal conflict about killing
King Duncan

Imperative language is used to construct tone in literary In non
literary works it is a call to action

*** Metaphor/Simile
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: metaphorsimile
:END:
Metaphor is a direct comparison between two things A simile is an
indirect comparison between two thing using “like” or “as” A metaphor
should be obvious

If a metaphor continues over a long period it is an extended metaphor

When you see a simile or metaphor you can analyse diction aswell

calculating and observant, however the connotations of “owl eyes”
suggest alertness aswell.

*** Personification
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: personification
:END:
A personification is giving non human things human charcteristics
e.g. “Every autumn the trees shed their leaves, and the red and yellow
and orange pieces fall onto the ground” -> “With every autumn comes the
sighs of frail oaks as they gently lay each of their red and orange and
yellow children to rest upon the soft forest floor in wintery, peaceful
silence”

Personification is most commonly used to: 1. make the setting come to
life -> creates an atmosphere 2. evoke emotion from the reader -> mood

**** Gaze
Gaze is where one is looking

There are 2 types of gaze:
- Demand gaze: refers to when the person is looking directly at the view
- Offer gaze: refers to when the person is not looking at the viewer

***** Analysis advice

Sometimes a visual text may show multiple people, who all have different gazes.
It is important to take note of their facial expessions are coupled with their gaze.
Ask yourself:
- How do their gazes influence my attention on other elements in text
- How do these gazes influence which subject I look first?

*** Alliteration
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: alliteration
:END:
The repetition of the same letter or sound at the start of adjacent
words

“The pitter patter of the rain” The repetition of the soft “p” mimics
the gentle sound of rain hitting the roof

“The jovial jazz group jammed late into the night” j creates musical
rhythm, increase pace and creates upbeat tone

“Jame's careful stare cut clean through the crude clothes, cosmetics and
caps and cardigans into the soul” The hard k reinforces the abstract
intensity of the “careful stare”

**** Analysis advice
- Alliteration mimics a real sound in order to immerse the reader
- When alliteration isn't used to mimic a sound, it can be used to evoke
  a certain feeling or reinforce abstract ideas
- Choose interpretation the best supports the writer's purpose

*** Assonance
Assonance is the repetition of vowels within words

“The man passed the gates, and pranced And ran in glee, so pleased He
leaped, as free As an eel in the deep blue sea”

Start is fast pace with assonance of a in short form constructing a
upbeat and carefree mood At and assonance of long e slows down pace and
illustrates the long term serenity of freedom

*** Consonance
Consonance is the repetition of specific consonants within words

“Isabelle slammed the closet shut” “d” and “t” suggest fast paced action

*** Rhyme

2 types of rhymes:

External: Occurs across different lines

Internal: Occurs in line

**** Analysis advice
- Sounds. Rhyme schemes create regular pattern of sounds = rhythmic

*** Prose

Prose, any structured text
These can include:
- Repetition
- Anaphora
- Listing / enumeration

**** Repetition

Purpose:
- Emphasis
- Repetition, syngerises with another technique to amplify effect.
  - String techniques together
- Can be used (varey rarely) to highlight obsession or desperation

***** Anaphora
When writer repeats the same word, or phrase at the start of successive sentences or lines.

****** Analysis advice
- Commonly used in speaches.
- Purpose: Amplify tone, atmosphere, or idea
- Also a rhetorical technique - persuasive

****** Example

We will not perish, we will not cower, we will not die

The anaphoric chanting of "we will not" creates a defiant tone that empowers the audience to defy

***** Enumeration (listing)

When author "lists" things
- Emphasises a particular idea, feeling or mental image.
  Not by repetition but by giving proof

****** Analysis advice
- Emphasis: Listing is almost always used to emphasise an image, idea or effect
- Get fancy. Use fancy terms such as "cumulative emphaisis" when analysing the purpose of listing.
  E.g. The listing of the visual imagery creates cumulative emphasis of the untidiness of the apartment

*** Poems

**** Stanza Length

Only ever analyse stanza length if special.

***** Short stanza

- Often creates shock
- Often used after a long stanza inducing shock

***** Long stanza

- Create flowing effect.


**** Line length

Only ever analyse if special

***** Short
- Normally used with enjambment
- Slow down pace


**** Line breaks

Line breaks are the spaces between stanzas

***** Analysis advice
- Transition
- Mid-line. Poets can also use line breaks when the line hasn't even finished.
  Disrupts the readers rhythm
  - Shock
  - Disorientation
  - Emphasise last word of line

**** Enjambment

When a line is cut off in the middle of a sentence without pause

***** Analysis advice

"From childhood's hour I have not been
As others were; I have not seen
As others saw; I could not bring
My passions from a common spring"

- Enjambment creates emphasis on last word in line.
- Main purpose is to create flowing, dynamic feeling for the reader
  Heavy use can also create chaos
- Increase pace
- It is a stylistic choice

**** Endstop
Opposite of enjambment. Concludes a line

***** Analysis advice

- Makes poem feel ordered.
- Slows down the pace

**** Caesura
Caesura is when a pause occurs in the middle of a line

***** Analysis advice
- Pace. In general, the purposes of caesura are to slow down the pace
- Tone. Eastablish a tone. Could create a contemplative and thoughtful tone.
  The start and stop nature of caesua can also create timid, uncertain tone
- Dramatic pause. Builds foreboading atmosphere, emphasis on words

**** Syntax

Syntax is the arrangment of words in a sentence

***** Disjoint Syntax
"She jumped and-" This has a fragmented sentence structure:
- Intensifies tone
- Heightened emotions

***** Parallel structure
Parallel syntax is the repetition of the same sentence - similar to anaphora

***** Long sentences
Depends on text

**** Polysyndeton and asyndeton

These techniques refer to the use of conjuctions

***** Polysyndeton
When there are extra conjunctions added to a sentence
E.g.

"and ... and ... and ..."
- Slows down pace
- Creates emphasis

***** Asyndeton
Asyndeton is the ommision of conjunctions

***** Analysis advice
- Polysyndeton and asyndeton are used to either speed up or slow down the pace of a sentence
- The can contribute to different tones, and reveal the state of mind of a character  or narrator
- Polysyndeton and asyndeton are commonly used with listing, so you can analyse both techniques together

**** Punctuation

***** Commas
Changes pace
- Creating tension
- Creating fragmented sentence structure
- Creating an uncertain tone
  
***** Ellipses

Ellipses are "..." used to indicate
- a pause in dialogue or thought
- a trailing thought
- an omission of information

****** Analysis Advice
- Create mystery by leaving out important information or dialogue
- Indicate a trailing thought or that a difficult decision is being made
- Create an uncertain tone in the character's or narrator's voice
