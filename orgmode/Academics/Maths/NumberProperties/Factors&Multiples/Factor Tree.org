A *factor tree* breaks a number into smaller factors until you are left
with *only prime numbers*.

*** Method 1: Pick any factor pair
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: method-1-pick-any-factor-pair
:END:
Find *two numbers that multiply to make your number*.

Example:

#+begin_src text
             24
            /  \
         6       4       ← factor pairs
        / \     / \
       2   3   2   2     ← prime numbers
#+end_src

The *leaves* are the numbers at the ends of the tree.

So:

*24 = 2 × 2 × 2 × 3*

--------------

*** Method 2: Keep dividing by small primes
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: method-2-keep-dividing-by-small-primes
:END:
Start with the smallest prime that divides the number.

Example:

#+begin_src text
60 ÷ 2 = 30
30 ÷ 2 = 15
15 ÷ 3 = 5
#+end_src

Since *5 is prime*, stop.

Therefore:

*60 = 2 × 2 × 3 × 5*

--------------

*** Important rule
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: important-rule
:END:
It does *not matter which factor pair you choose*.

As long as you keep splitting until *all the leaves are prime*, you will
get the same prime factorisation.

*Factor pair → split → split → stop when everything is prime*
