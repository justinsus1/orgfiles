Subjective:

My illustration, “One Australia for all of us” depicts a majestic serene
view of The Three Sisters, located in the Blue Mountains. This reflects
on my journey in the year 7 Knox school camp, during term 2. There were
3 stone formations sticking out bizarrely, however still fantastical and
captivating. There, we were told stories of the dreaming, the wonderful
ancestor spirits who created the land, and the different rituals of the
first nations people by an affable indigenous elder, who guided us
through the lands. The amazing native flora and fauna that exist, such
as the Wollemi pine, which was fossilized 91 million years ago, however
still thriving in the Blue mountains. Moreover, the amicable native
animals  of the Koala and lyre birds sharing refuge on the oak/gum tree
capture how the Australian spirit is to look out for each other and have
mutual respect. 

Cultural:

My illustration, “One Australia for all of us” depicts the beautiful
scenery of the 3 sisters in the blue mountains to critique British
colonialism. The 3 sisters featured in the artwork symbolises Australian
identity and indigenous culture as it is a significant landmark with
cultural value within the indigenous community, this evokes amazement to
illustrate the aesthetic and cultural value of the 3 sisters,
emphasising Indigenous identity. Additionally, the symbolism of the
superb lyrebird illustrates the British colonisation as the ability of
the superb lyrebird is to copy, this is a metaphor for the assimilation
of the First Nations People. The eye within the branch, the superb
lyrebird is perching on constructs fear as it evokes a sense of
surveillance, this synthesises with the metaphor of the superb lyrebird
to illustrate the atrocities committed. Ultimately, my illustration
utilises various Australian signs to critique the atrocities committed
by British colonialism
