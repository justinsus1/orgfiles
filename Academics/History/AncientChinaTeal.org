* Explain how the geography and natural features of ancient China impacted its development

The geography and natural features of ancient China drastically effected the social and the military development of China. The Chinese empire orginated from the 2 main rivers of China, being the Yellow river, and the Yangtze river, around these 2 rivers, civilisation developed as the valleys nearby were exceptionally fertile leading to agriculture and settlement within these sites. However, the Yellow river, considered to be the birthplace of Chinese civilisation, was also known as 'China's sorrow' wreaking havoc quite often, bringing the destruction of families and agriculture. Conversely, this empowered China, forcing large amounts of people to cooperate, and unite under a central leadership, birthing Chinese civilisation. Furthermore, surrounding China was the Himalayas, the Altai mountains, the Mongolian Plateu, and the Tibetian Plateu. These mountains gave China a geographic advantage against its enemies, able to rely on these mountains for defense against outsiders, developing immense military might, eventually becoming one of the global powerhouses. Ultimately, the geography and natural features of ancient China drastically effected the social and military development of China.

* Describe how Chinese society was organised and administered

The organisation and administration of China is unique and drastically different from other cultures. To begin with, China was primaryily split into 7 unique classes being Shi, the scholars and officials. The Eunuchs, castrated men who served the palace. Nong, farmers (most of the population). Gong, Artisans responsible for producing tools and materials such as silk. Soldiers. Shang, Merchants, often extremely wealthy but often looked down upon as they did not provide to society. Nubi, enslaved people. Furthermore, China was ruled by the emperor, and power was passed to the eldest son of the emperor, this system is known as a dynasty. The emperor was expected to be responsible and care for the people, this ideaology is seen through the 'Mandate of Heaven'. The 'Mandate of Heaven' is essentially a title that is given to the emperor which grants him the right to rule over China, if during an emperor's rule, a natural distaster were to occur, this would be seen the the 'Mandate of Heave' being stripped from him, and gave people a chance to overthrow the current dynasty. However, with such an immense population, the emperor cannot simply rule by himself. Therefore, the emperor elects officials, who would each look over a part of China, this is known a bureaucracy, similar the Three levels of government within Australia, with the Federal, State, and Local government. Orginially these officials would be selected from noble families. However, in ~587-605 C.E. the 'Ke Ju' system was introduced, a meritocracy, where people from all of China would go through 3 rounds of testing, each one of them, elimating a large amount of people, until the government recieves the 20 most capable people in China, and these people would be appointed an offical role in the government, contrary to Western systems. Ultimately, due to China's isolation, it developed a unique administratoin and organisation sysem.
